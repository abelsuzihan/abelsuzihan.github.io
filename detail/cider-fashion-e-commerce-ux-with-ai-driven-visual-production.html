Role: UX & Product Design Lead

Founded in 2020, Cider grew into a global fashion e-commerce brand for Gen Z shoppers. As the product scaled, our team worked across product strategy, UX, and visual design to bring the brand’s expressive character into an experience that still makes browsing and buying clear. We approached the app as more than a storefront: it needed to support discovery, merchandising, and the relationship with customers beyond checkout.

Four Evolutions, One Home Experience

Across four iterations, the home experience evolved to balance Cider’s visual expression with the merchandising team’s need to feature timely stories and customers’ need to find relevant products. The merchandising area became shorter, personalized content moved higher on the page, and modules grew more flexible. The most focused version centered on one video-led story, with a product grid leading directly into shopping.

Redesigning the Journey with Evidence

Product evidence helped us connect design exploration to customer and business outcomes. We mapped the journey from a customer’s first impression of Cider through product discovery and purchase, then tested changes to hierarchy, navigation, and content to understand how each decision affected that path. Usability feedback was considered alongside browse depth, product exposure, add-to-bag, conversion, repeat purchase, and retention. Together, these signals helped us prioritize improvements across the journey instead of optimizing screens one at a time.

On product detail pages, we reduced competing copy and gave product imagery, style, and size selection a clearer hierarchy. Contextual AI surfaces useful product information when it can help with a decision, while keeping the page focused.

After these changes, related-product visibility increased by 20%, You May Also Like GMV grew by 30%, and product conversion rose by 4 percentage points.

Complete the Look shows how pieces work together, not just how they look individually. Outfit suggestions make complementary items easier to discover, while campaign-linked product details carry a collection’s styling into the shopping journey.

By making related pieces part of the styling story, the feature made cross-selling feel more relevant and contributed to a 5–10% increase in average order value.

AI Try-On builds a complete styled look around the item a customer has chosen, helping them picture how it might work as part of an outfit. Members can redeem points for daily image-generation credits, making the feature easy to try and giving them a reason to keep exploring styles.

Collection pages offer three browsing modes: a focused two-column view, a denser three-column grid, and an immersive single-column feed. Each suits a different intent, from comparing products to following an editorial story.

A consistent product structure lets customers change views without having to relearn how the page works.

Shop was rebuilt around a three-level category structure to make a large assortment easier to navigate. In testing, we weighed clear orientation against faster access to products, using total product exposure across collections as a guiding measure.

The selected direction favors efficient discovery, with a simpler interface, more legible information, and a clearer visual path through categories.

In the bag, complementary suggestions appear alongside items customers have already chosen. Product and styling context makes an additional purchase feel relevant, while keeping these prompts secondary to the route to payment.

Checkout steps and key information were also clarified, reducing friction at the point where customers are ready to complete their order.

AI search and recommendations support product discovery, while Me brings orders, points, and membership into a clearer structure. Shared visual patterns tie these areas together, keeping the experience consistent from the first browse through post-purchase tasks.

Across the redesign, our role was to make inspiration and shopping work together. Visual design carries Cider’s character, product structure makes discovery easier, and testing shows where the experience needs to change. The result is a clearer connection between browsing, purchase, and the services customers return to, supported by a system the team can continue to evolve.

AI-Powered Visual Production

In 2023, our team began exploring generative image tools such as Midjourney to broaden pattern exploration. I encouraged experimentation, while keeping art direction and review at the center of the process.

As Stable Diffusion and Flux improved, the team tested them at different points in content production: creating virtual sets for still-life photography, combining product and portrait imagery, training LoRA models to recreate specific environments, and using inpainting to preview designs on models before physical samples were ready. Each method was evaluated for the control and consistency it offered, not just the speed of generating an image.

Further experiments in ComfyUI explored virtual models, face replacement, and batch-generation workflows. Although these tests had not entered commercial production, they revealed where AI could save time and where image quality, consistency, and review still required close attention.

These trials let us test ideas earlier, from previewing patterns before samples existed to replacing backgrounds and exploring virtual try-on. We found the tools most useful when they shortened a specific feedback loop and left the team in control of the final result. That remains our standard for bringing new technology into content production and customer experience.
